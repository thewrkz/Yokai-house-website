The House

Where old legends
meet Pacific salt air.

In Japanese folklore, yōkai are the spirits that live in the in-between — mischievous, fierce, protective, strange. Yokai House was built on that idea: a private, appointment-first studio in San Diego where centuries-old tattoo tradition meets modern technique and a Southern California soul.

Every piece starts as a conversation and a hand-drawn design. No copy-paste, no rush jobs — just bold lines, smooth shading, and tattoos that read from across the room and still reward a close look.

What We Do

Four paths. One spirit.

01 入墨

Traditional Irezumi

Full sleeves, back pieces and bodysuits rooted in Edo-period composition — wind bars, waves, peonies and the beasts that ride them.

02 新和

Neo-Japanese

Classic subjects reimagined with saturated color, heavier contrast and a little SoCal swagger. Koi that look like they could bite.

03 黒墨

Blackwork

Bold, graphic, all black. Sumi-e brush textures, ornamental patterns and dark-folklore pieces that age like fine steel.

04 細線

Fine Line

Delicate single-needle work — tiny spirits, botanical studies and script that whispers instead of shouts.

Good to Know

Questions,
answered.

Do you take walk-ins?

We're appointment-first, but we post open flash days on Instagram. When the lanterns are lit, walk-ins are welcome for flash.

How much does a tattoo cost?

Our shop minimum is $150. Larger pieces are quoted by the session or by the piece after your consultation. Deposits go toward your final session.

How old do I need to be?

18+ with a valid government-issued photo ID, per California law. No exceptions.

Can you cover up or rework an old tattoo?

Absolutely. Japanese and blackwork styles are fantastic for cover-ups. Send a clear photo of the existing tattoo with your inquiry.

How should I prepare for my session?

Sleep well, eat a solid meal, hydrate, and skip alcohol the night before. Wear clothes that give easy access to the placement.
